# Operation Albia (11 August – 29 August 1942)

Operation Albia (Croatian: *Operacija Albia*) was an Axis anti-partisan operation conducted between 11 and 29 August 1942 in the Biokovo mountain region of occupied Dalmatia. Its aim was to encircle and destroy the Partisan "Josip Jurčević" Battalion, which controlled the area between the Cetina and Neretva rivers, and to reassert Italian and Ustaša control over the Makarska–Vrgorac hinterland.

## Background

By the summer of 1942, Partisan units had linked the areas around Livno–Duvno–Kupres–Bugojno with forces in Bosanska Krajina, Herzegovina, and Dalmatia. In this region, the roughly 720-strong "Josip Jurčević" Battalion held the territory between the Cetina and Neretva rivers, aside from Italian and Ustaša strongholds along the coast and the Omiš–Imotski, Makarska–Vrgorac and Metković–Čapljina routes. Partisan bases were concentrated in remote hamlets on the Biokovo and Rilić mountains, and Partisan units also held sway over much of Brač, Hvar, and Korčula.

Concerned that continued Partisan activity threatened Italian communications and risked the movement spreading into central Dalmatia and western Herzegovina, the Italian Supreme Command "Slovenia-Dalmatia" (Gen. Mario Roatta) ordered the 6th Army Corps (headquartered in Dubrovnik) to prepare an operation to recover lost positions and crush the Partisan units in the area.

## Planning

The plan, codenamed "Albia," was drawn up by the 6th Corps commander, General Renzo Dalmazzo, and approved by General Roatta. The operational area was bounded by the Cetina river to the west, the Neretva to the east, the coast to the south, and a line running from Cista Provo through Studenci, Imotski, Bijača, Nova Sela, and Vid to Metković in the north.

The operation was to proceed in two phases:
- **Phase one** (16–27 August): units of the "Bergamo" Division would clear the Biokovo massif, while units of the "Messina" Division cleared the Vrgorac–Metković–Ploče–Zaostrog quadrilateral.
- **Phase two**: the "Josip Jurčević" Battalion would be encircled and destroyed in the Brikva–Velika Kapela–Sokolić area, while Italian naval units blocked any Partisan withdrawal to the islands of Hvar and Brač.

## Course of the Operation

Italian forces struck early, on 11 August, when the 2nd Battalion of the "Bergamo" Division's 26th Infantry Regiment, supported by a tank company, began clearing the area between Šestanovac and Grabovac and pushed on toward Zagvozd, which Partisans had taken on 9 August. Companies of the "Josip Jurčević" Battalion checked the advance near the hamlet of Svaguše, but the Italians reached Zagvozd the next day, forcing the Partisans back onto Vlaka.

Fighting around Lovreć and Studenci against the 1st Proletarian Brigade and the Central Dalmatian Detachment delayed the main assault on Biokovo, and on 16 August the "Bergamo" Division could not launch its planned full-strength attack on the north-western part of the massif. That same day, units of the "Messina" Division and Chetnik forces began clearing the Vrgorac–Metković–Ploče–Zaostrog quadrilateral from the southeast. Fresh Blackshirt reinforcements (the 29th Battalion) were committed on 18 August after the situation north of Biokovo failed to improve for the Italians.

Over the following days, Italian columns advanced along the Makarska–Tučepi–Podgora–Drašnice–Igrane axis and the Zagvozd–Lovrinčevići axis, supported by artillery and air attacks, while the "Josip Jurčević" Battalion continued to resist in the Turija and Staza passes. On 27 August, heavy Italian attacks on both axes cost the Partisans 24 dead and 10 wounded as they lost control of the Turija pass, but elsewhere the Italians made no progress.

On 29 August, acting on orders from the Zone Staff, the bulk of the "Josip Jurčević" Battalion (about 650 fighters) broke through the Italian lines near Grabovac without loss and reached the Aržano area; The third company "Vid Mihaljević" could not break out with the main body and instead dispersed into small groups across eastern Biokovo. The operation's final phase therefore failed to achieve its objective of destroying the battalion.

During and after the sweep, Chetnik units under Petar Baćović, operating alongside the Italians, burned numerous villages and massacred civilians in the Zabiokovlje area, most notably in the massacre of 29 August 1942. Baćović reported to Draža Mihailović that seventeen villages had been burned and that his men had reached the sea near Makarska with minimal losses of their own.

## Outcome

Italian communiqués described the operation as a success and claimed the destruction of the opposing Partisan formations, but this proved inaccurate: the "Josip Jurčević" Battalion escaped largely intact, and Partisan units regrouped soon afterward around the hamlet of Šošići, absorbing new recruits from Kozica, Dragljane, Ravča, Kljenak, Rašćane, Župa, and Zagvozd. By mid-September these forces had grown into a new Partisan brigade.

Reported Axis losses were 16 dead and 39 wounded in the "Bergamo" Division, 1 dead and 2 wounded in the "Messina" Division, and 38 dead, 39 wounded and 50 missing among the Chetniks.
