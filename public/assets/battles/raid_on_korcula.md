# Raid on Korčula (22–24 April 1944)

The Raid on Korčula (Serbo-Croatian: *Desant na Korčulu*) was an amphibious operation carried out by the Yugoslav Partisan 26th Dalmatian Division against the German garrison of the island of Korčula. Launched from Vis on the night of 22/23 April 1944, it was one of the most successful Partisan amphibious raids of the war and served as a form of revenge for the division's earlier defeat on the island during Operation "Herbstgewitter II" in December 1943.

## Background

By spring 1944, the German 2nd Panzer Army was forced to hold numerous isolated garrisons on the Adriatic islands to secure sea lanes and deny them to the Partisans and Allies. These scattered garrisons were vulnerable to sudden raids. The Partisan 26th Division, operating from its base on Vis, planned a decisive strike against the German garrison holding Korčula, defended by elements of the 118th Jäger Division.

## Plan of Attack

The plan called for the landing force to be shipped from Vis under cover of darkness on the night of 22/23 April and put ashore, undetected, at two opposite ends of the island — north and south. After eliminating local strongpoints, both columns were to converge on the main German defensive position around the town of Blato, which dominated the island, before withdrawing with captured equipment and prisoners.

To secure the operation, the staff of the 26th Division ordered a diversionary attack on German positions on the nearby island of Mljet on 21 April, intended to draw the mobile reserve of the German 118th Jäger Division away from Korčula. The deception fully succeeded: while German reinforcements were engaged on Mljet, the main Partisan force crossed from Vis to Korčula unopposed.

## Course of the Action

Landings on Korčula were completed by 05:00 on 22 April. The southern column — four battalions of the 1st and 11th Dalmatian Brigades with the mountain battery — attacked from the south, while the northern column, built around the 12th Dalmatian Brigade, struck from the north. The assault, delayed from 06:30 to 08:00 while units concentrated, was aided by heavy fog that allowed the Partisans to approach German positions undetected. By the end of the day, the outer defenses of the Blato stronghold had been overrun (except for a position on Hill 181), a German battery was captured, and the German strongpoint at Vela Luka was eliminated.

At 23:00, the main Partisan forces launched a concentric assault on the German position at Blato itself, while smaller detachments blocked German forces on the eastern side of the island. The Germans, however, held out from a well-fortified position protected by bunkers and minefields, particularly around the heights of Sv. Vito and Gospe od Zdravlja, with organized machine-gun and artillery fire, and repelled all night attacks in the hope of relief.

The decisive assault came on 23 April at 11:00, when Partisan units advanced methodically behind artillery and mortar fire, destroying German strongpoints one by one. The last German resistance was broken by around 16:00. Over the following day and night, units of the 26th Division evacuated their prisoners and captured equipment back to Vis.

## Outcome

The raid was a major Partisan success. German losses on Korčula amounted to 297 killed and 439 captured, along with the loss of four 75mm howitzers, four anti-tank guns, ten heavy machine guns, 26 light machine guns, five 81mm mortars, five light mortars, and large quantities of infantry weapons. In the diversionary fighting on Mljet, the Germans lost a further 96 killed and 46 captured. The 1st Dalmatian Brigade lost its 2nd Battalion commander, Marko Lagator, along with the battalion's deputy political commissar Ivan Pivac and deputy commander Mile Marković, among other casualties.

The action demonstrated the Partisans' ability to destroy any single German strongpoint in the Adriatic, forcing the German command onto a more defensive posture and greater concentration of forces on the islands. Supreme Commander Marshal Tito commended the participating units in an order issued on 28 April 1944, and the Allied Commander-in-Chief Mediterranean, General Henry Maitland Wilson, sent Tito a message praising the operation. In the following months, the 26th Division carried out similar raids against Šolta, Hvar, and twice against Brač.
